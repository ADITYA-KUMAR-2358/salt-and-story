FICTIONAL DEMO DATA. The speaker is invented; the recipe is a classic.

Now, the pie. Everybody wants to rush the crust and you can't. Two and a half cups of flour, a teaspoon of salt, a cup of cold butter cut in small, and cold water, maybe six or seven spoons, a little at a time till it just holds together. Rest it in the icebox an hour. Peel six big apples, Granny Smiths, and slice them thin, toss them with three quarters of a cup of sugar, a spoon of cinnamon, a little nutmeg and a squeeze of lemon. Roll out the bottom, pile the apples high, dot with butter, put the lid on top, crimp it, and cut little slits so it can breathe. Bake at four hundred for fifteen minutes, then drop to three fifty for forty-five more, till the juice bubbles through. The whole house smelled like this pie every Thanksgiving, and your grandfather would sneak a corner before dinner. Let it cool. I know it's hard, but let it cool.
